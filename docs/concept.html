M0 Concept · third game idea · 23 Oct 2026

Moth

A calm 3D platform-puzzler about a small cat made of dusk smoke, wandering a hand-painted valley and waking its sleeping spirits.

Creative direction by Fable, scope and tech by the director. Working title. We dropped Fable's first title, Soot and Sun, because "soot" points straight at a famous Ghibli creature.

Our honest take

Yes, it's a strong idea, with one twist and one condition.

The twist: the cat is made of shade and the world is made of light. Smoke thins in sun and thickens in shadow, so the game is about moving between light and shade, never about speed or danger.

The condition: "high graphic" has to mean strong art direction, not high polygon count. The Ghibli feeling is painterly and stylised. A small team built by AI can reach that look. Photoreal fur and a huge open world are out of reach.

What makes it good: a black cat is instantly lovable, smooth 3D movement is something Bloom and Still Water never gave you, and "shadowish cloudy" works as a movement idea, a puzzle idea and a mood at the same time.

The risks, and how the concept answers them:

  • Challenges turn into stress. 3D platformers drift toward timers, deadly falls and retries, which break the calm feel. Here falls float you back and nothing has a timer.
  • A black cat in a dark world can't be read. So the world is bright and warm, and nothing else is as dark as the cat.
  • Exploration games are often played once. Short errands, keepsakes and a home nook that fills up give you a reason to come back without pressure.
  • It's a much bigger build than our puzzles. The art, the cat and the camera are the expensive parts. See Building it.
The cat

A small cat shaped from dusk smoke

In full sun Moth thins to a soft grey wisp with a visible outline. In shade it thickens to deep velvet black. The tail is a slow trailing wisp that never quite ends. The silhouette is clean and round, with a slightly big head and paws. No fur.

The eyes are the one bright point: two warm amber ovals, always the brightest thing on the cat and often the brightest thing on screen.

Personality. Curious, unhurried, a bit proud. Helps because it's nosy, not because it's a hero. Never afraid. Silent: purrs, chirps and body language only.

Other name ideas: Sumi (ink) and Umbra (shade). Fable recommends Moth: warm, a little funny, and drawn to light.

Always readable

  • Silhouette. Levels are built bright and warm around the cat's paths.
  • Rim light. A thin warm edge that follows the sun, so the shape keeps a line in shade.
  • Eye glow. Always on, so you can find the cat with no HUD marker.

Little things to love

  • Long stretch after 4 seconds still.
  • Loafs in a sun patch after 10, smoke thinning.
  • Kneads moss and cushions. Tracks moths, lifts one paw.
  • Naps in sunbeams. Sits right where you wanted to look.
  • Shakes off after water.
Movement

Four core moves, seven smoke abilities

One stick and two buttons on phone and desktop. Camera follows gently, and you can drag to look.

Stick / WASD move A jump, again in the air B tap to bap, hold for the smoke action

Core moves

Walk, trot, run

First build

How far you tilt the stick sets the speed. The smoke trail gets longer as Moth speeds up. Soft pad-pad steps that change with the surface.

Jump

First build

High, with a long float at the top. A small smoke ring on takeoff and a hush of air.

Double jump

First build

Moth bursts into a puff and reforms a little higher. A soft "whuff" and one woodwind note.

Paw bap

First build

A quick swipe with a squishy "pap". Knocks things off ledges, rings bells, flips switches, pats spirits awake. This is the move you'll use most after walking, so it gets the richest feedback: wobble, a flick of particles, a tiny camera nudge.

Smoke abilities, one new one per level

Thin

First build

Hold B by a fence or crack in sunlight. Moth thins to smoke and seeps through, like a slow breath out.

Opens shortcuts, locked gardens, "how do I get in there" moments.

Wall-shade

Level 2

In a wall's shadow, Moth flattens into a shadow and slides along the wall, even upward. Like ink moving on paper.

The sun's angle decides which walls you can use.

Pounce-bap

Level 3

Hold B, release while moving: a bigger slap that pushes crates and rolls fruit.

Simple physics puzzles, like a melon onto a plate.

Box squeeze

Level 3

Jump into any box, pot or basket and Moth always fits. A satisfying "plomp".

Boxes ride the wind, float on water, get lifted by spirits.

Cloud glide

Level 4

Hold A at the top of a double jump. Moth puffs into a little cloud and drifts down, steerable.

Long gentle crossings, roof landings, no fear of falling.

Purr

Level 5

Hold B while still. A low purr spreads out, with phone haptics. Wakes spirits, calms ripples, opens flowers.

The softest move in the game.

Sun-soak

Level 7

Rest in a sun patch until Moth is almost see-through, then walk along a beam of light as a bridge.

Late routes where you follow light itself.

What you do in a level

Return the ember-seeds, wake the spirit

Each level is a small place with one thing asleep. You wander, bap, climb and seep through gaps to find a few ember-seeds, small warm seeds that glow like Moth's eyes. You carry one at a time, floating above the tail, to the sleeping Hearth Spirit in the middle of the level.

Each seed wakes the place a little more: a lantern lights, a stream starts, blossoms open. With the last seed the spirit wakes fully, the place breathes out, and the path to the next level opens.

One bright goal at a time

The seed you're heading for is the only glowing thing. Others stay dim until you're close.

Nothing to fail

Fall off an edge and you puff into a cloud and drift back. Water just makes Moth a sulky grey cloud that paddles out. No lives, no damage, no timer.

Optional high routes

A thin trail of fireflies marks a harder path to a keepsake (a ribbon, a bell, a hat). Keepsakes change looks, never power.

A reason to come back

A windowsill nook at the valley inn fills with returned things. Every woken spirit changes the skyline you see from it. Levels take 8 to 15 minutes: one errand and a nap.

HUD: at most two numbers, and only when they matter. Seeds placed (amber) and, on a high route, keepsakes found (blue).

Art direction

A hand-painted gouache world with one ink-black cat in it

Light

Every level leans golden, even noon. Soft shadows with wide edges. Big painted cumulus skies. One light shaft per scene at most, pointing at the current goal.

Shapes and surfaces

Round, chunky buildings, a little crooked, as if drawn quickly with love. Layered watercolour textures with paper grain. Foliage sways as painted clumps. Tall grass bends around the cat and in the wind.

Camera

Third person, pulled back, gently lagging. Default views land on composed shots. Reaching a vista slowly pulls back, so the frame itself is the reward.

UI

Hand-inked and paper-coloured. Tutorial lines appear as small brush text in the world, next to the thing. No icons on screen in normal play. The pause menu is a sketchbook.

Sound leads

Solo piano, clarinet, nylon guitar, light strings, a bowed glass tone for spirits. Ambience always; melody fades in while you carry a seed; a warm chord when it lands; the spirit's theme only at full wake. Paws sound different on wood, stone, tile and grass. Smoke uses breath sounds, never whooshes.

Other references

Journey (one goal glowing ahead), A Short Hike (small kind world, optional peaks), Alba (quiet errands that matter), Ori (fluid air movement, without its difficulty), Ghost of Tsushima (wind as a guide).

How we stay original to Ghibli

We borrow feelings only: wind in grass, quiet pauses, food warmth, painted skies, small spirits, gentle melancholy at dusk, a valley that reacts. Our spirits are our own: round clay-and-ember figures like kiln-fired pots with moss. No bus, no soot balls, no big forest creature, no witch's cat. Moth has no white markings or bow and is made of smoke. No names, places or characters from any film.

How we differ from other cat games

Stray is a realistic cat in a neon city with chases and stealth. Little Kitty, Big City is a realistic cat doing comedy chores in a town. Moth is a smoke creature whose body changes with light, in a painted country valley, moving by thinning, shadow-sliding and gliding. No chases, no humans to annoy. The puzzles are about light and shade.

The first minute

What you'd see in the greybox

Mill Terrace at dawn. Each tutorial line appears in the world near the thing it's about, eight words or fewer, one new move at a time.

0–10 s

Black screen, a single purr. Fade up on a dewy terrace, mist on the river. Moth is loafing in a sun patch and opens its eyes as you move. "Stretch. Then wander."

10–25 s

A low stone wall with a pot on it and a moth circling. "Jump. Tap again up high." Landing on the wall plays a soft chord.

25–40 s

The pot sits at the edge. "Bap it." It wobbles, tips and breaks softly into petals and an ember-seed, the only glowing thing. The seed floats to the tail and the melody fades in.

40–55 s

A fence with sunlight pouring through the slats. "Breathe through the sun." Hold B, Moth thins and seeps through.

55–60 s

The dim Hearth Spirit by the waterwheel. Walk up, the seed drifts in, one lantern lights, the wheel creaks once. No text. Moth sits. You know the whole game.

Building it

Doable, if we spend effort on look and feel, not detail

The smoke cat is also a technical gift. A body of soft smoke hides the hardest thing in a cat game, which is four-legged animation. Legs can be short procedural strokes, the body a soft blob, the tail a rope of wisps. A realistic furry cat would need a proper rig and dozens of hand-made animations.

For us, code is the easy part. The hard parts are below.

Movement feel

Smooth 3D movement is mostly tuning: speed curves, a short grace time after leaving a ledge, jump buffering, landing squash, camera lag. This is the Feel milestone and needs many short play rounds from you.

Camera

The top cause of frustration in 3D platformers. The calm answer is a gentle automatic camera with set framings per area, and dragging to look as an option.

Phone controls

A virtual stick plus buttons in 3D feels cramped on a phone. Either we lead with keyboard or gamepad and keep phone playable, or we design a simpler phone scheme from the start.

Art assets

Terrain, props and the cat take the most work. Blender can run headless here, so props and terrain can be generated by script. The cat is procedural by design. A bought prop pack or an artist could speed up content later.

Phone performance

Grass, soft shadows and fog are expensive on phones. A quality setting and a resolution cap from day one.

Download size

A web 3D world has to stay small to open from a link. Target under 20 MB for the first load.

Milestones, and what you'd play at each gate

M1 Greybox

Mill Terrace in plain shapes. Placeholder smoke cat with walk, jump, double jump, bap and Thin. Gentle camera, one seed puzzle, falls that float you back.

Gate: is moving around already pleasant?

M2 Feel

The real smoke cat (body, wisps, eyes, idles), painterly shader, wind grass, sky, first music and paw sounds. Tuned controls and camera.

Gate: do you love moving this cat? Engine decision.

M3 Content

Levels 2 to 4 with their new abilities, keepsakes, the home nook, saving.

Gate: do you want the next level?

M4 Polish

Levels 5 to 8, phone performance pass, accessibility, outside testers.

Gate: ready to show people.
